# Codzee — UX Document

**Version 1.0**
**Status: Active**
**Purpose: Define the experience Codzee delivers to every visitor. This document governs how the site behaves, how visitors move through it, and what they feel at every step. Every interaction decision gets checked against this file.**

---

## The Experience We Are Designing For

Codzee is not designed for the average visitor. It is designed for the most impatient, easily distracted version of the right visitor. If that person can move through the site without friction, without confusion, and without effort — everyone else will have an even better experience.

This is the standard every UX decision is held to.

---

## Three Experience Layers

Every decision on this site operates across three distinct layers. All three must be present. Missing one collapses the experience.

**Layer 1 — Feeling**
How the site looks and presents itself. Calm, premium, professional in that order. Governed by the Design System.

**Layer 2 — Flow**
How the visitor moves through the site. Never confused. Never stuck. Never having to think about the next step. The path is so clear and natural that moving forward feels effortless. The visitor always feels in control — but the site is designed so the right visitor cannot leave without satisfying their curiosity.

**Layer 3 — Response**
How individual elements behave when the visitor interacts with them. Every hover, every click, every scroll transition responds in a way that feels satisfying and considered. The response is subtle enough that the visitor feels it without consciously noticing it. Satisfaction without spectacle. The site never draws attention to itself — it draws attention to the visitor's problem and Codzee's ability to solve it.

## Core UX Principles

These eight principles govern every decision on every page.

**1. Arrival feels made for you**
The first two seconds on any page — especially the homepage — must make the right visitor feel this was built specifically for their situation. Not a generic welcome. A specific recognition.

**2. Pattern breaking keeps the brain engaged**
The brain scans layout structure in milliseconds and pattern-matches against everything it has seen before. If it matches a known pattern — SaaS template, agency portfolio, Framer theme — the brain files it as "another one of those" and disengages before a word is read. Codzee breaks structural and visual patterns deliberately so the brain stays active and curious. Pattern breaking is a UX tool, not just a design choice.

**3. Every section earns its place**
Nothing exists to fill space or impress. Every section has a job. Every section delivers something the visitor needed without knowing they needed it. If a section cannot be justified by what it does for the visitor — it does not belong.

**4. Curiosity pulls forward, never pressure**
Every section ends slightly incomplete. Not frustratingly — just enough that the next section answers the question the current one opened. The visitor moves forward because they want to, not because they were pushed. CTAs are invitations, never demands.

**5. The visitor always feels in control**
The site is designed to guide without appearing to guide. The visitor makes every choice themselves. They never feel herded, pushed, or sold to. The path is clear but the decision is always theirs.

**6. The right visitor reaches contact**
Every path through the site ends at one destination — Contact. Some paths are short. Some are long. Both end at the same place. The longer path arrives warmer. The site is designed so the right visitor cannot leave without at least reaching the contact page.

**7. Wrong visitor leaves with a lasting impression**
Not every visitor is the right fit. That is fine. A construction worker, a student, someone who landed here by accident — they leave having experienced something they remember. That memory has value. It builds recognition over time.

**8. Nothing breaks the spell**
Slow loading, unexpected behavior, a layout that jumps, a link that goes somewhere wrong, a form that feels like work — any of these pulls the visitor out of flow instantly. For the impatient visitor that is not a minor inconvenience, it is the exit. Performance and micro-interaction quality are UX decisions, not technical afterthoughts.

## Visitor Journey

### The Chain

Every page opens a door to the next. The visitor always feels in control. They are never pushed. Each step is a small natural choice — their own idea, not Codzee's suggestion.

### Any Page Is An Entry Point

The journey works regardless of where someone enters. Every page stands alone and connects to the chain. SEO, backlinks, and direct traffic all land on different pages. Each one must deliver the full experience from that entry point.

### The Destination

Every path through the site ends at Contact. The site does not have multiple goals. It has one destination and many paths to it.

## Navigation

**Structure:** Logo — Work — Services — About — Contact

**Home is not in the navigation.** The logo returns home. This is universal behavior every visitor already knows. A Home link is a template habit, not a decision.

**Contact is visually distinct from the other links** — not a full button, not a plain link. Positioned between the two. Distinct enough to be found when the visitor is ready to act. Quiet enough to never scream for attention during the journey. Detail governed by UI.md.

**The nav is always visible.** Hidden navigation creates micro-friction. Codzee is built for the most impatient visitor. A menu trigger that requires an extra click to see any links violates the effortless journey principle.

**The nav is persistent across page transitions.** It is the one constant element the visitor holds as pages change around them. It never flickers, reloads, or jumps.

---

## Page Transitions

**No hard reloads.** The site feels like a single continuous experience. When a visitor navigates between pages the transition is seamless — no white flash, no jarring reload, no sense of leaving and arriving.

**The nav persists.** Content changes beneath the nav smoothly. The visitor never feels like they left.

**Transitions are fast and directional.** Not decorative. The transition communicates movement — you are going somewhere — without performing or drawing attention to itself.

**This is not infinite scroll.** Infinite scroll removes control, destroys SEO, and breaks the warehouse principle when Codzee holds many projects. Seamless transitions deliver the feeling of continuity while preserving page identity, SEO value, and visitor control.

## Entrance and Scroll Behavior

**Entrance — space first, then revelation.**
When the visitor lands the page feels almost empty for a half second. Clean. Quiet. Then elements arrive in sequence — not all at once. The site assembles itself quietly, like it is preparing specifically for this visitor.

Entrance sequence:
1. Space — half second of near-emptiness
2. Nav — quiet, already present
3. Primary heading — arrives slowly
4. Supporting text — a beat later
5. Any CTA — last, because it is the least important thing on arrival

**Scroll — revelation continues.**
Every section below the fold reveals as the visitor scrolls into it. Not all at once. Primary element first. Supporting elements follow with slight delay. The visitor feels the hierarchy before they read the content.

**The visitor's pace is the pace.**
Nothing animates on a timer. Nothing is automatic. Scroll drives everything. This creates the feeling that every reveal is happening specifically for this visitor at this moment.

**Animation never repeats.**
Once revealed, always present. Re-animating on scroll back up breaks the feeling that this experience was made for the visitor specifically.

## Interaction Response Rules

**Responses are felt, not seen.**
Every interactive element responds to the visitor's presence. Hover states, active states, transitions between states. Each response is subtle enough that the visitor feels it without consciously noticing it.

**Nothing performs.**
The site does not show off its interactions. No physics, no magnetic cursors, no dramatic effects. The interaction serves the visitor — it does not entertain them.

**Consistency builds trust.**
Every interactive element responds with the same timing logic, the same easing, the same level of subtlety. The brain learns the pattern and relaxes. That relaxation is calm.

**Timing:**
Fast interactions — 300ms. Base interactions — 600ms. Entrance and reveal — see Animations.md Section 7 for the authoritative timing table (differentiated by element role). These values are non-negotiable. Fast animations feel cheap. Considered timing feels premium.

## Mobile Experience

**Mobile first.**
Every page is designed for the smallest screen first. Desktop is a scaled and enhanced version of mobile — not the other way around. This is both a design principle and a development instruction.

**Same principles, adapted constraints.**
The three experience layers — feeling, flow, response — apply identically on mobile. The visitor on a phone should feel the same calm, the same premium quality, the same effortless journey as the visitor on a desktop.

**Touch is the primary interaction.**
Hover states are desktop enhancements, not the primary interaction. Every interactive element works perfectly without hover. Touch targets are large enough to be hit without effort.

**Nothing breaks on small screens.**
The pattern breaking, the typographic hierarchy, the asymmetric layouts — all of these must hold on mobile. A design that only works on desktop is an incomplete design.

## What This Site Eliminates

These things do not exist on Codzee because each one breaks one or more core UX principles:

- Popups or overlays that interrupt the visitor's flow
- Auto-playing video or audio that the visitor did not trigger
- Infinite scroll (removes control, destroys SEO)
- Hidden navigation behind a menu trigger
- Aggressive CTAs that push rather than invite
- Forms that ask for more than they need
- Any animation on a timer that the visitor did not trigger
- Content that exists to impress rather than to serve

---

## The Test For Every UX Decision

Before any interaction, flow, or structural decision is made:

1. Does it serve the most impatient, easily distracted version of the right visitor?
2. Does it keep the visitor feeling in control?
3. Does it move the right visitor forward without pushing them?
4. Does it maintain the feeling, flow, and response layers simultaneously?
5. Does it break a pattern the brain would recognize — without breaking a pattern the visitor expects?

If any answer is no — the decision is wrong.
