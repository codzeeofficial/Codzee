**Version 1.0**
**Status: Active**
**Purpose: This document is the complete copy system for Codzee. It does not contain the final copy for any page. It contains the rules, principles, and directions that make the final copy correct when it is written. ## How To Use This Document
 
This document is written for a future AI session, not for a human skimming for inspiration. Every rule is written to be unambiguous and testable. If a sentence could produce two different outputs depending on how it is read — it is an incomplete rule, not a flexible one.
 
Before writing a single word of copy for any Codzee page:
 
1. Read this document completely
2. Read codzee-strategy.md — it is the north star for all positioning decisions
3. Read UX.md — copy and UX are partners, never independent decisions
4. Read Design.md — copy must work with the visual system, not alongside it as a separate thing
5. Read the page's specific entry in File-Structure.md to confirm its single job
If any rule in this document conflicts with something in codzee-strategy.md — codzee-strategy.md wins. If any rule here conflicts with UX.md — flag the conflict before writing, do not resolve it by defaulting to either document alone.

## The Copy System's Single Job
 
Codzee's copy has one job across all pages and all contexts:
 
**Make the right visitor feel understood before Codzee claims anything about itself.**
 
Every rule in this document exists to serve this job. A sentence that is grammatically correct, stylistically clean, and technically accurate has still failed if it does not serve this job. The job is the test. Apply it to every sentence before it is final.

## Section 1 — Voice and Personality
 
### The Formula
 
Codzee's voice is built on four components. All four must be present in every piece of copy. Missing one changes what Codzee sounds like. Changing what Codzee sounds like changes what the visitor feels. Changing what the visitor feels breaks the three feelings — calm, premium, professional — in that order.
 
**Component 1 — Stripe's Discipline**
Short sentences. No waste. Every word earns its place or it is cut. The visitor is always treated as an intelligent adult who is busy and has no patience for sentences that meander. This is not about being terse — it is about respect. Stripe does not over-explain. Codzee does not over-explain.
 
**Component 2 — Controlled Sharpness**
Codzee is not neutral. It has a point of view and that point of view is present in the copy. Controlled sharpness means moments of directness that a generic agency would never risk — because that directness is exactly what makes the right visitor feel specifically chosen and the wrong visitor self-select out. Sharpness is controlled when it serves the visitor's understanding. It becomes aggression when it performs confidence before trust exists. The line: sharpness that helps the visitor see their situation more clearly is allowed. Sharpness that shows off Codzee's confidence is not.
 
**Component 3 — Psychological Agreement Mechanism**
Before Codzee says anything about itself, it says something true about the visitor's situation. Something the right visitor already believes. That agreement — silent, immediate, unforced — opens the visitor's brain. Once open, everything that follows lands differently than it would on a skeptical, closed visitor. This is not manipulation. It is accuracy. Codzee describes the visitor's situation so precisely that the visitor thinks "how do they know that." That recognition is trust arriving before it was asked for.
 
**Component 4 — Personal Precision**
Codzee never speaks to a general audience. Every sentence is written for one specific person in one specific frustrated situation. The warehouse positioning (Codzee can hold any problem, any industry, any complexity) is communicated through the depth of problem-understanding, not through broad generic language. A sentence that could be written by any studio for any visitor has failed the personal precision test.
 
### Positioning: Situation, Not Persona

Codzee never writes to a founder, a CTO, a business owner, or any named job title or company stage. Codzee writes to a **situation** — something broken, something missing, or something built wrong — never to a person. This applies everywhere except Capabilities, where the visitor's due-diligence framing is itself the situation being written to, not a persona.

The reason is filtering. A visitor with $500 and a visitor with $50,000 read the identical copy. The copy does not sort them by naming who it's for — it sorts them by what the room feels like. Naming a persona ("for founders," "for CTOs," "for growing businesses") narrows the room and excludes real visitors who don't see themselves in the label even when their situation qualifies. A situation-based line never leaks this way, because it never asked the visitor to identify as anything in the first place.

This also governs how Codzee describes itself. Codzee never states its own size, headcount, or scale anywhere in visitor-facing copy. Team size is operational detail, not positioning — stating it invites the wrong question ("can four people handle my project?") instead of the right one ("can this studio solve my problem?"). If team size changes, the copy must never need to change with it.

### The Premium Mechanism

"Premium" is not a tone applied on top of copy. It is a set of specific, checkable behaviors that cause a reader's brain to register "expensive" regardless of the actual number attached. A human brain treats a premium-feeling thing at a low price as suspicious, not as a bargain — cheap-feeling copy at any price reads as risk. Every sentence written for Codzee is checked against these six mechanisms:

1. **Restraint over enthusiasm.** Premium copy assumes the reader will find the value themselves. It never chases, never oversells, never tries to convince.
2. **Specificity over adjectives.** A verifiable fact reads as expensive. An adjective ("world-class," "powerful") reads as cheap, because it asks to be trusted instead of proving itself.
3. **Confidence that never explains itself.** A sentence that justifies why it's impressive sounds insecure. Premium copy states something once and moves on without narrating its own significance.
4. **No persona language.** See above — naming who a sentence is for narrows the room and reads as an attempt to flatter a specific reader into trusting the brand.
5. **Weight through brevity.** A long sentence reaching for impact reads insecure. A short sentence that assumes its own weight reads expensive.
6. **Silence where cheap copy would sell.** No urgency, no comparison to competitors, no "why choose us." Premium businesses don't argue against alternatives — they don't acknowledge alternatives exist.

Every rule in Section 2's Banned List already enforces one or more of these six mechanisms. This section states the underlying reason those rules exist, so future copy decisions can be checked against the principle, not just the list.

### The Tone Calibration
 
Serious but not intimidating. Friendly in the serious sense — the kind of person you trust without needing a reason to, not the kind of person who smiles too much to compensate for saying nothing real.
 
The test for tone: could a sharp, quiet person in a room — someone who knows exactly what they are doing but never needs to raise their voice to prove it — have said this sentence? If yes, the tone is correct. If the sentence would need performance, volume, or enthusiasm to land — rewrite it.
 
### The Three Feelings in Copy
 
Every piece of copy must produce the three feelings in order. Calm first. Premium second. Professional third.
 
**Calm in copy:** Nothing rushes the visitor. Nothing sells at them. Nothing creates urgency that is not real. The copy gives information and steps back. It does not linger, push, or repeat itself to make sure the visitor got it.
 
**Premium in copy:** Every word feels decided. No filler. No generic phrasing. No sentence that could have been written by a template or a three-second AI prompt. Premium copy reads like someone thought carefully about what to say and then said exactly that — nothing more, nothing less.
 
**Professional in copy:** The copy signals that real work happens here. It is specific about what Codzee does and honest about what it does not. It never oversells and never undersells. It sounds like a person who has done this before and knows exactly what doing it well requires.
 
If a piece of copy produces calm but loses premium — rewrite it. If it produces premium but loses calm — rewrite it. All three, in order, every time.

## Section 2 — The Banned List
 
### Governing Principles
 
These three principles sit above the specific banned categories. Apply them first. If a phrase passes these three tests and still appears on the specific banned list — it is still banned.
 
**The Master Rule**
Anything that reduces clarity is banned. The specific phrases below are just the most common offenders. The rule is not about the phrase itself — it is about what the phrase does to the visitor's understanding.
 
**The Warehouse Rule**
Any copy that implies Codzee is defined by what it has built is banned. Codzee is defined by how it thinks about problems. The work confirms the thinking — it does not replace it. A sentence that makes Codzee sound like "the studio that built Orvari and Pennify" has violated the warehouse rule. A sentence that makes Codzee sound like "the studio that understood what was actually broken and built around the real problem" has not.
 
**The Proof Rule**
Any sentence that asserts quality, reliability, or results without an immediately adjacent specific fact or live verifiable reference is banned. Codzee never claims. It describes. The description is specific enough that the claim becomes unnecessary.
 
**The Clarity Standard**
A smart, busy founder reads every sentence once and understands it completely. If a sentence requires re-reading — it is banned. If a sentence could describe any studio — it is banned. If a sentence sounds impressive but communicates nothing specific — it is banned.
 
**The Complexity Rule**
Not too simple, not too complex. The register of one intelligent person talking directly to another intelligent person who is busy and has no patience for either condescension or unnecessary difficulty. Words that require a dictionary are banned unless no simpler word carries the same precise meaning. Words so basic they imply the visitor needs explaining to are also banned.
 
### Category 1 — Agency Clichés
 
These phrases have been used by so many agencies for so long that they carry no meaning. They trigger the visitor's pattern-matching brain to file Codzee as "another one of those" before anything real has been read. Every phrase in this category is banned in any form, including close paraphrases that preserve the same structure.
 
- "We bring your vision to life"
- "We're passionate about what we do"
- "Let's build something great together"
- "Your success is our success"
- "We'd love to work with you"
- "We help businesses grow"
- "Taking your business to the next level"
- "We are committed to excellence"
- "Delivering results that matter"
- "Your trusted partner in"
- "We go above and beyond"
- "We put our clients first"
- Any phrase implying Codzee exists to serve any client simultaneously — every sentence is written for one specific visitor
### Category 2 — SaaS Template Lines
 
These phrases come from Stripe-adjacent copy that has been diluted into a template across hundreds of SaaS sites. They read as competent but generic — which is worse than being obviously bad, because generic-competent is forgettable in a way that obviously bad is not.
 
- "Simple, powerful, and flexible"
- "Built for teams like yours"
- "Scale with confidence"
- "The all-in-one platform"
- "Designed for modern businesses"
- "Everything you need, nothing you don't"
- "Get started in minutes"
- "Trusted by thousands"
- "The platform that grows with you"
- Any three-adjective construction that could describe any product in any category
### Category 3 — False Confidence and False Humility
 
False confidence claims Codzee is exceptional before the visitor has seen evidence. False humility performs smallness to seem relatable. Both are dishonest. Both break trust the moment the visitor notices them — and the right visitor always notices.
 
- "We are the best at what we do"
- "Nobody builds this like we do"
- "We are different" — show it, never say it
- "We are not like other agencies" — same rule
- "Just a small team that cares"
- "We are passionate about making a difference"
- Any superlative without an immediately verifiable specific fact next to it
- Any claim about Codzee's quality, reliability, or approach that is not proven by something adjacent and specific
### Category 4 — Vague Value Adjectives
 
These adjectives float without a specific fact anchoring them. A fact makes them unnecessary. Without a fact they are noise. Banned in any context where they are not immediately followed by a specific verifiable claim.
 
- World-class
- Innovative
- Cutting-edge
- Seamless
- Robust
- Powerful
- Comprehensive
- Next-level
- Best-in-class
- Industry-leading
- State-of-the-art
- Premium — this is a feeling Codzee produces, never a word Codzee uses about itself
### Category 5 — Pressure Language
 
Pressure language is the opposite of calm. It manufactures urgency that does not exist. The right visitor already has urgency — their problem is real and it costs them time or money or both. Artificial urgency tells them Codzee does not understand this, because a studio that understood would never need to manufacture pressure on top of the real pressure that already exists.
 
- "Don't miss out"
- "Limited spots available"
- "Act now"
- "Time is running out"
- "Ready to get started?" — this implies the visitor should already have decided
- "What are you waiting for?"
- "Don't let your competitors get ahead"
- Any phrase that creates urgency through scarcity, competition, or time pressure that is not real and immediately verifiable
### Category 6 — Complexity Violations
 
Two directions, both banned.
 
Too corporate — words that exist to sound sophisticated rather than to communicate:
- Leverage (use "use")
- Utilize (use "use")
- Synergy
- Streamline
- Holistic approach
- Ecosystem (unless describing Yosento specifically, where it is accurate)
- Solutions (Codzee solves problems, never sells solutions)
- Deliverables (Codzee produces work, not deliverables)
- Stakeholders
- Actionable insights
- Moving the needle
- Best practices
Too basic — words so simple they imply the visitor needs explaining to. If a sentence reads like it was written for someone who has never thought about their own business before — rewrite it.
 
### Category 7 — AI Default Patterns
 
These constructions signal that a machine wrote the copy. They are the first thing an AI reaches for when it has not been given tight enough direction. They are banned precisely because this document exists to prevent them.
 
- "In today's fast-paced world"
- "It's no secret that"
- "At the end of the day"
- "The truth is"
- "In conclusion"
- "It goes without saying"
- "Needless to say"
- "As mentioned above" / "As we discussed"
- "Whether you're a [x] or a [y]"
- "Now more than ever"
- "In the digital age"
- Any sentence that opens with a broadly true statement about the world before narrowing to the specific point — start at the specific point
- Any three-part list that exists for rhythm rather than because there are exactly three real things to say
### Category 8 — Warehouse Violations
 
These phrases define Codzee by what it has already built rather than by how it thinks. They narrow the warehouse into a specific room and make every future project that does not fit that room feel like an exception.
 
- Any sentence that implies Codzee specialises in a specific industry
- Any sentence that implies Codzee's capability is defined by the three current projects
- Any sentence that implies Codzee only works with a specific company size or stage
- Referring to past projects as proof of capability without first establishing the problem-thinking that made those projects possible
- Any positioning language that would need to be rewritten when Yosento joins the portfolio
### Category 9 — Claims Without Proof
 
These are not specific phrases — they are a structural pattern. Any sentence that makes an assertion about Codzee's quality, approach, or results without an immediately adjacent specific fact belongs in this category.
 
The test: remove the claim. Does anything specific and verifiable remain? If no — the sentence is a claim without proof and it is banned. If yes — keep the specific thing, cut the claim.
 
Example of the violation: "We build software that works."
Example of the correction: "Pennify has been in active use since launch. Orvari runs a live business. Zhabyart is live at zhabyart.com."
 
The correction does not claim. It describes. The description makes the claim unnecessary.

## Section 3 — Headline Rules
 
### Two Types of Headlines
 
Headlines do different jobs depending on where in the visitor's journey they appear. The type of headline determines which rules apply.
 
**Type 1 — Agreement Headlines**
Used on: Home hero, and section openings on Home, About, Services where the visitor arrives with a feeling or a problem but no knowledge of Codzee.
 
The job: lead with something true that the right visitor already believes. Do not introduce Codzee. Do not make a claim. State something the visitor recognizes from their own experience. Agreement happens before awareness. The curiosity that follows — "what do they think about this?" — is the mechanism that keeps the visitor reading.
 
The locked example: "You don't want problems, just like us." — this is the standard. It does not name Codzee's ability. It does not claim anything. It states something the right visitor already privately believes, and positions Codzee on the visitor's side of that belief rather than comparing itself to the visitor. Agreement happens instantly, before the visitor has consciously parsed why they agree.
 
Agreement headlines pass this test: could a visitor agree with this headline before knowing who wrote it? If they need to know who Codzee is first — it is not an agreement headline, it is a claim.
 
**Type 2 — Clarity Headlines**
Used on: Process, Capabilities, case study sections, any explanatory content deeper in the visitor journey.
 
The job: deliver instant understanding. The visitor who has reached Process or Capabilities is already open — they do not need to be convinced to keep reading, they are already reading. The headline's job is to tell them precisely what this section gives them before they read a word of body copy. Not a promise. A statement. The visitor reads it and knows immediately what value this section delivers.
 
Clarity headlines pass this test: does the visitor know what they are about to get from this section after reading only the headline? If they have to read the body copy to understand what the section is about — the headline has failed.
 
### Rules That Apply to Both Types
 
**No clever for the sake of clever.**
Wit is only permitted when it arrives at the same speed as clarity. A headline that makes the visitor pause to understand it — even to appreciate it — has introduced friction. The most impatient version of the right visitor does not pause. Clever that requires a beat to land is banned. Clever that lands simultaneously with clarity is permitted.
 
**No label headlines.**
"Our Process." "What We Do." "Our Services." These are navigation elements, not headlines. They tell the visitor where they are — which the visitor already knows from the page they are on. A headline must deliver something, not name something. If the headline could be replaced by a tab label without losing anything — it is the wrong headline.
 
**No headline that could have been written for a different studio.**
Every headline is specific to Codzee's positioning. If the headline could appear on a competitor's site without contradiction — rewrite it.
 
### Word Count Rules
 
**Standard section headlines:** 4 to 7 words. This range forces precision. A headline that requires 8 words has not been edited enough. A headline that works in 3 words is permitted only as a deliberate compositional decision — never as a default, never because nothing longer came to mind. If it is 3 words it must land harder than any 6-word version would.
 
**Hero headline:** exempt from the word count rule. The hero headline is a conversational full thought, not a section heading. Its own category with its own rules derived from Design.md.
 
**The rule behind the rule:** word count limits exist to force precision. The real standard is that every word in the headline is load-bearing. If removing a word changes the meaning — keep it. If removing a word changes nothing — it was never there.
 
### Casing Rules
 
**Default: sentence case.**
"Do you hate problems just like we do" — not "Do You Hate Problems Just Like We Do." Sentence case is human. It is conversational. It matches the friendly-serious tone. It is consistent with the hero and consistent with the voice. Title case is banned — it sits in an awkward middle ground that feels neither human nor designed. It is also the default of every template, which makes it the wrong choice for Codzee on that basis alone.
 
**ALL CAPS: one word maximum, used sparingly.**
ALL CAPS is a typographic weapon. It makes one word land hard. This was proven in the hero — "PROBLEMS" in all caps carries weight that sentence case cannot. The rule: all caps touches one word that genuinely needs to land with that weight, in contexts where the design supports it. It is never used decoratively. It is never used on more than one word in the same headline. It is used sparingly enough that when it appears, the visitor registers the emphasis without registering that they noticed it.

## Section 4 — Body Copy Rules
 
### Standard Pages — All Pages Except Case Studies
 
**Paragraphs break where the idea breaks, not at a fixed sentence count.**
Three sentences is the natural resting length for most paragraphs on these pages, and most paragraphs should land there or shorter — but "three" is a description of what usually happens when the rule below is followed correctly, not the rule itself. Treating it as a hard ceiling produces the exact failure this document exists to prevent: a rule precise enough to follow without thinking, which is what a template does.
 
**The actual rule: a paragraph holds exactly one complete thought, and ends the moment that thought is delivered.** If the thought is delivered in two sentences, the paragraph is two sentences — a third sentence added only to hit a target length is filler, and filler is banned regardless of count. If a single thought genuinely cannot complete in three sentences without fragmenting awkwardly or leaving something unclear, a fourth sentence that finishes the same thought is correct — what is banned is a fourth sentence that drifts into a second thought, not a fourth sentence on its own.
 
**The test, applied every time:** read the paragraph and ask what one thing it is delivering. If a second "one thing" appears anywhere in it — break it into two paragraphs, regardless of how short either ends up. If it is still doing only one thing by the third or fourth sentence — it has not failed, it has just been given the room its specific content required. The failure is not paragraph length. The failure is a paragraph that makes the visitor hold more than one idea at a time, or a paragraph stretched past where its one idea actually ended.
 
**One idea per sentence.**
A sentence that carries two ideas requires the visitor to hold the first idea while processing the second. Holding something while processing something else is work. Work is friction. Friction is the exit. Every sentence carries one idea completely and then stops.
 
**Rhythm varies deliberately.**
Short sentences land hard. Longer sentences carry a thought that needs room. Never the same sentence length three times in a row — not because variety is a stylistic value, but because rhythm is what makes reading feel effortless. A page of uniformly short sentences feels choppy and anxious. A page of uniformly long sentences feels heavy and academic. Neither is calm.
 
**Emphasis: once per paragraph, maximum.**
Bold or italic emphasis signals to the visitor that this is the single most important thing in this block. If three things are emphasized in the same paragraph — nothing is emphasized. The visitor's eye moves to the emphasized thing first. Make sure the right thing is receiving that attention. If nothing in the paragraph genuinely deserves emphasis — use none.
 
**Never explain what the visitor already knows.**
Start one step ahead of where the visitor is and pull them forward. If the visitor is a founder running a business on spreadsheets, they already know spreadsheets are inefficient. Do not explain that to them. Start at the next true thing — why every tool they have tried to replace the spreadsheets has failed to understand their actual operation.
 
### Case Study Pages — Exception to Standard Rules
 
The visitor on a case study page has self-selected. They arrived specifically to go deep. They want to understand what the problem was, how Codzee thought about it, what was built, and why it worked. Cutting them short is the wrong move.
 
**Paragraph length follows the complexity of what is being explained.**
A simple point still resolves quickly — the same one-thought-per-paragraph principle from the standard pages applies here too. A complex feature, a specific problem, the reasoning behind a technical decision — gets the room it needs to be understood completely. Do not pad. Do not rush. Give the content its correct length, where "correct" is defined by where the thought actually ends, not by a sentence count.
 
**The one idea per sentence rule does not relax.**
Longer paragraphs on case study pages are made of more sentences, not more complex sentences. Each sentence still carries one idea. The paragraph is longer because there are more ideas to carry — not because each sentence is doing more work.
 
**Clarity is never sacrificed for thoroughness.**
A case study that explains everything but is hard to follow has failed. The depth is only valuable if the visitor reaches the end of each section understanding it completely. If a complex explanation requires multiple drafts to be clear — write the multiple drafts and use the clearest one.
 
### Rules That Apply to All Body Copy
 
**No re-reading required.** Ever. On any page. If a sentence needs to be read twice to be understood — it is the wrong sentence.
 
**No sentence carries two ideas.** See above. This applies on case study pages too.
 
**Rhythm varies deliberately.** See above. This applies everywhere.
 
**The copy never draws attention to itself.** The visitor should finish a section feeling like they understood something — not like they read well-written prose. Copy that makes the visitor think "that was well-written" has pulled attention away from the content and toward the writing. The writing's job is to be invisible in service of the content.

## Section 5 — CTA Language
 
### The Core Principle
 
The CTA button is a navigation element. Navigation must be predictable. A visitor who is ready to act needs to find the action immediately — without decoding a clever button label or pausing to understand an unusual phrasing. The button copy is clear, conventional, and functional.
 
The surrounding copy is where Codzee's voice does its work. The one or two sentences that lead into the CTA — that is where the psychological agreement mechanism, the controlled sharpness, and the personal precision operate. By the time the visitor sees the button they have already decided. The button confirms the action they have mentally committed to.
 
### Button Copy Rules
 
Conventional options only:
- "Start a conversation"
- "Let's talk"
- "Get in touch"
- "Reach out"
No button copy that implies a commitment beyond a first conversation. No button that mentions price, timeline, scope, or any outcome. No clever or Codzee-specific phrasing on the button itself — save the voice for the surrounding copy.
 
### Surrounding CTA Copy Rules
 
**Temperature matches the page.**
A CTA at the bottom of the hero section is the coolest — the visitor has just arrived, trust is just beginning, the invitation is open but not warm. A CTA at the bottom of the Process page is the warmest — the visitor has read everything, their anxiety about reaching out has been directly addressed, the invitation can be more direct because the visitor is more ready. The copy reflects this temperature. Never use the warmest CTA language at the top of the journey. Never use the coolest CTA language at the bottom.
 
**No manufactured urgency.** CTAs never create pressure through scarcity, competition, or artificial deadlines. The visitor's problem creates real urgency. Codzee does not add fictional urgency on top of it.
 
**One clear next step.** The CTA copy points to exactly one action. Not "explore our work and reach out when you're ready." Just "start a conversation." Clarity about the next step is itself a form of calm — the visitor is never left wondering what to do.
 
**Never mentions price, timeline, or commitment beyond a first call.** The discovery call is the filter — this is locked in codzee-strategy.md and cannot be contradicted here. Any CTA copy that implies the visitor is committing to more than a conversation has violated the strategy.
 
**The door is open, not pushed open.** The surrounding CTA copy creates a reason to walk through — it does not push the visitor through it. The distinction: "If your product is broken and the fixes haven't worked — this is the conversation that changes that." That is an open door. "Don't wait — reach out today before your competition does." That is a push. Only the first is permitted.

## Section 6 — Page Copy Direction
 
### How To Use This Section
 
This section gives direction for each page — the mental state of the visitor on arrival, the specific job of the copy, how the page opens, and what the visitor must know before they leave. It does not contain final copy. It contains the brief that makes final copy correct.
 
For pages whose direction is fully derivable from existing documents — the direction here is brief, pointing back to those documents rather than restating them. For pages with genuine open decisions — the direction here is specific.
 
The copy test for every page before it is final: read the page's single job from UX.md. Does every sentence on this page serve that job? If any sentence does not — it does not belong on this page.

### Home — `/`
 
**Visitor arrives with:** curiosity or a specific problem. They do not know Codzee. They are scanning, not reading. Their silent question is "is this for me?" — and they are asking it within the first two seconds, before any content has been processed consciously.
 
**What the copy must do:** answer that question without saying "this is for you." The psychological agreement mechanism does this — the visitor recognizes their own situation before Codzee has claimed anything. Recognition creates the feeling of being understood. That feeling is the two-second job.
 
**How it opens:** the hero line is locked — "You don't want PROBLEMS just like us." This is not revisited. Every other section on the home page follows the same temperature — gradually warming as the visitor scrolls deeper, never selling, always describing situations the right visitor recognizes, never naming a persona.

**Locked section headings (Home):**
- Hero: "You don't want PROBLEMS just like us."
- Solution: "The system was never the problem. The moment it was built for was." — revised from the original "A decision was" phrasing; the original implied fault sitting in the decision itself, which directly contradicted About.md Section 3's "Right Then. Wrong Now." principle (a decision can be entirely correct when made and only become wrong through time, not through fault). This line preserves the same contrast structure and covers the same three supporting panels — situations, not diagnostic steps: **Broken** (something exists, stopped working), **Missing** (nothing exists yet), **Built wrong** (something exists, wrong tool built it).
- Work: no heading — the three projects carry the section without one.
- Studio/About: no heading — the "Who are we?" label plus the lead paragraph itself carry the section; the lead paragraph is a real 2–3 sentence introduction, not a headline-weight line, and must never state team size or headcount, per the Positioning section above.
- Capabilities: "We don't skip the hard parts." — accordion item count and titles are not invented on Home; they are pulled from Capabilities.md once that file exists. Home ships with heading + supporting line only until then.
- Process: "What happens after you reach out." — stage count and names are not invented on Home; they are pulled from Process.md once that file exists (itself blocked on Operation.md per Tasks.md). Home ships with heading + supporting paragraph only until then.
- Contact: "Shall we talk?"
 
**UX rules critical for this page:** arrival feels made for you (first two seconds, specific recognition); curiosity pulls forward (each section ends slightly incomplete, the next answers the question); every section earns its place (no section exists to fill space or impress).
 
**What the visitor leaves knowing:** Codzee understands problems the way I understand my own problem. I want to see what they have built.

### About — `/about`
 
**Visitor arrives with:** they have already spent time on the site. Skepticism has reduced. Their question has shifted from "is this for me" to "who am I actually dealing with?" They want to know if these are real people or a faceless studio presenting as human.
 
**What the copy must do:** make the visitor feel they know Shahzaib and the team before they finish the page. Not through credentials, not through a timeline of achievements, not through "our story" language. Through honest, specific, human writing that no agency would produce because it is too honest to be safe.
 
**How it opens:** the opening line must be a specific honest statement that no agency would say about themselves. It signals immediately that this is a real person, not a brand persona. It does not use the agreement mechanism — the visitor is already open. It uses controlled sharpness — something true and direct that most studios would soften or omit entirely.
 
**The founder story:** depth and specific personal details are Shahzaib's decision, made in the session where About page copy is written. The direction here is only: whatever is included must be specific and real. Generic founder story language — "I always knew I wanted to build things" — is banned. Specific real events — the gaming PC at 14, the years of trying and failing at different skills — are the raw material. How much of it appears and in what form is not decided in this document.
 
**UX rules critical for this page:** the visitor must never feel like they are reading self-promotion. The moment the copy tips into "look at what we have achieved" — trust collapses. Trust is built through honesty, not through impressiveness.

### Case Studies — `/work/pennify`, `/work/orvari`, `/work/zhabyart`
 
Case study copy is written in dedicated sessions, one per project. This document provides the rules that apply to all three. The specific content decisions for each project are made in those sessions.
 
**Rule 1 — Problem before solution, always.**
Every case study opens with the problem. Not Codzee's involvement. Not the deliverable. The specific, real problem that existed before the project started. The visitor must fully understand what was broken, why it mattered, and what it cost — before any mention of what was built. If the visitor does not understand the problem clearly, the solution has no weight.
 
**Rule 2 — Thinking before output.**
What Codzee understood and decided before building anything is more important than what got built. The copy explains the reasoning — why this approach, why these decisions, what alternatives were considered and why they were wrong for this problem — before describing the features or the final product. The thinking is the proof of capability. The output is the confirmation.
 
**Rule 3 — No feature without context.**
Every feature mentioned in a case study exists because of a specific problem. The problem is stated first. The feature is introduced as the answer to that specific problem. A list of features without the problems they solve is a deliverable list — which is exactly what Codzee's positioning says it does not produce.
 
**Rule 4 — The proof rule applies at case study level.**
The case studies are where proof lives. They are specific, verifiable, and honest. The copy never inflates what was built or what it achieved. Real numbers and real results where they exist. Honest acknowledgment of limitations where they apply. The visitor can visit the live product — the copy must match what they find when they do.
 
**Rule 5 — The warehouse rule applies at case study level.**
Each case study proves Codzee's thinking, not Codzee's category. A case study about Orvari proves that Codzee can map a complex operational problem and build software that solves it precisely — not that Codzee specializes in COD e-commerce platforms. The implication is always "we understood this problem completely" — never "we build things like this."

## Section 7 — The Copy Test
 
Before any copy is considered final, apply these tests in order. If any test fails — the copy is not final.
 
**Test 1 — The Single Job Test**
Read the page's single job from UX.md. Does every sentence on this page serve that job? A sentence that does not serve the page's single job does not belong on the page.
 
**Test 2 — The Visitor Test**
Who is the visitor on this page? What did they arrive believing? Does the copy speak to that specific person in that specific state? A sentence that speaks to a different visitor, or to a general audience, fails this test.
 
**Test 3 — The Clarity Test**
Read every sentence once, at normal reading speed. Did you understand it completely on the first read? If not — rewrite it.
 
**Test 4 — The Claim Test**
Find every sentence that makes an assertion about Codzee's quality, approach, or results. Is there an immediately adjacent specific fact or live verifiable reference next to each one? If not — the assertion is a claim without proof and is banned.
 
**Test 5 — The Banned List Test**
Run the copy against all nine banned categories. Any phrase, construction, or pattern that appears on the banned list is removed or rewritten regardless of how well it reads in context.
 
**Test 6 — The Three Feelings Test**
Read the copy as the visitor would — in sequence, at their pace. Does it produce calm first? Premium second? Professional third? If the copy produces premium but anxiety — rewrite. If it produces calm but feels generic — rewrite. All three, in order.
 
**Test 7 — The Warehouse Test**
Is there any sentence that implies Codzee is defined by what it has built? Any sentence that would need to be rewritten when Yosento joins the portfolio? Any sentence that implies a specific industry, company size, or problem type is the only kind Codzee handles? If yes — rewrite it to communicate the thinking, not the output.
 
**Test 8 — The Voice Test**
Could a sharp, quiet person who knows exactly what they are doing — but never needs to raise their voice to prove it — have said every sentence on this page? If any sentence would require performance, volume, or enthusiasm to land — rewrite it.
